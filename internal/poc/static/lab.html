What this build actually does

  • Mint and bid work. A bidder mints fake sats from the demo mint and locks them to a 2-of-2 SIG_ALL condition (its own wallet key and the committee). A SIG_ALL signature covers the whole transaction — inputs and outputs — so the lock can only ever be spent as one of the exact outcomes the bidder pre-authorized, never redirected.
  • The bidder publishes its authorizations with the bid. Before bidding, the bidder signs both outcomes (settle to the seller, refund to itself) and deposits them. The coordinator commits only the compact digests and signatures on chain; the bearer templates stay in the coordinator's store. This is what lets the bidder leave.
  • Refund works when a bid is displaced. The coordinator submits the bidder's pre-authorized refund transaction, or parks the returned proofs for the displaced bidder to collect. Either way the money returns to the bidder's own wallet key. Verified end to end against the live mint.
  • Seller settlement works at close, with no bidder tab open. The seller commits a wallet payout key when listing. At close the coordinator submits the winner's pre-authorized settle transaction from its stored deposit and the payout lands on that key, so the seller can spend it. Verified end to end against the live mint.
  • The bidder holds the funds until it bids; the coordinator holds them until the outcome. The bidder's proofs are its own until it bids. Between the bid and the outcome the coordinator holds the locked proofs (they travel with the deposit so it can act alone), and under SIG_ALL it can only send them to the destination the bidder pre-authorized — the digest commitment is what makes that checkable, not a promise.
  • A late bid pushes the close out. Consensus extends the end to three minutes after any bid that lands in the last three minutes, up to thirty minutes in total (anti-snipe). So an auction asked to run for sixty seconds will usually close about three minutes after its last bid, not after sixty seconds.
  • The sats are worthless. The mint is a FakeWallet mint. Nothing here is redeemable.
  • The committee is one key, not FROST. Four CometBFT validators run on one host, with no FIPS transport. One operator controls all of them. The coordinator's half of each SIG_ALL spend goes through a single port today; FROST will sit behind that port, and it does not yet. This is not production and not a compliance claim.
  • The monitor is a commit timeline, not a block explorer. The API exposes consensus timestamps for auctions and bids, not the height each event landed in. The page says so where it matters instead of inventing block contents.
